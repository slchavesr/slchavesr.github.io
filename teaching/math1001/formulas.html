Length, mass, volume, and time

MeasurementConversion
Length1 foot = 12 inches; 1 yard = 3 feet; 1 mile = 5,280 feet
Metric length1 kilometer = 1,000 meters; 1 meter = 100 centimeters
Length across systems1 inch = 2.54 centimeters (exact); 1 mile ≈ 1.609344 kilometers
Weight/mass1 pound = 16 ounces; 1 pound ≈ 0.45359237 kilograms; 1 kilogram ≈ 2.20462262 pounds
US customary volume1 gallon = 4 quarts = 8 pints = 16 cups
Metric volume1 liter = 1,000 milliliters
Time1 hour = 60 minutes = 3,600 seconds; 1 day = 24 hours
Area1 square yard = 9 square feet; 1 square foot = 144 square inches

For squared units, square the linear conversion factor. For example, 1 yd² = (3 ft)² = 9 ft².

Temperature: Fahrenheit and Celsius only

\[C=\frac{5}{9}(F-32)\]
\[F=\frac{9}{5}C+32\]

For a temperature change, use \(\Delta F=\frac95\Delta C\) and \(\Delta C=\frac59\Delta F\). Do not add or subtract 32 for a temperature difference.

Examples: 68°F = 20°C; −10°C = 14°F; an increase of 10°C is an increase of 18°F.

Unit rates and dimensional analysis

\[\text{Unit rate}=\frac{\text{total quantity}}{\text{number of units}}\]

Multiply by conversion fractions so the unwanted units cancel. For speed, 1 mph = 5,280 ft / 3,600 s ≈ 1.46667 ft/s.

Percentages and budgeting

\[\text{Percentage}=\frac{\text{part}}{\text{whole}}\times100\%\]
\[\text{Percent change}=\frac{\text{new}-\text{old}}{\text{old}}\times100\%\]
\[\text{New amount}=\text{original}\left(1+\frac{p}{100}\right)\]
\[\text{New amount}=\text{original}\left(1-\frac{p}{100}\right)\]

For successive changes, multiply the factors. A 10% increase followed by another 10% increase produces a total increase of 21%, not 20%.

\[\text{Savings}=\text{income}-\text{expenses}\]
\[\text{Sector angle}=\frac{\text{category amount}}{\text{total}}\times360^\circ\]
\[\text{Profit margin}=\frac{\text{selling price}-\text{cost}}{\text{selling price}}\times100\%\]

Markup percentage instead uses cost as the denominator.

Simple and compound interest

\[I=Prt\]
\[A=P(1+rt)\]
\[A=P\left(1+\frac{r}{n}\right)^{nt}\]

Here P is the principal, r is the annual interest rate as a decimal, t is time in years, and n is the number of compounding periods per year. Common values: annually 1, quarterly 4, monthly 12, weekly 52, daily 365.

\[t=\frac{\ln(A/P)}{n\ln(1+r/n)}\]

Loans, mortgages, and annuities

For a loan principal P, monthly interest rate i = r/12 (with r as a decimal), and N monthly payments:

\[M=\frac{Pi}{1-(1+i)^{-N}}\]
\[\text{Total interest}\approx MN-P\]

Use the rounded monthly payment for estimated total interest if the problem explicitly asks you to do so. Actual loan schedules may adjust the final payment.

\[\text{Amount financed}=\text{price}-\text{down payment}\]
\[\text{Total house cost}=\text{down payment}+MN\]

For equal deposits D at the end of each compounding period, periodic interest rate i, and N deposits:

\[FV=D\frac{(1+i)^N-1}{i}\]

For a loan with unpaid simple interest before repayment, accrued interest = Prt. If that interest is capitalized, the new repayment principal is P + Prt.
